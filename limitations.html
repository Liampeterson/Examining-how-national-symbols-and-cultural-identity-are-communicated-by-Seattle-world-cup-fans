Limitations

Project Limitations and Areas for Future Research

Like any short field study, this project's scope, sample, and point of view shaped what could and couldn't be observed.

Demographic Bias

When considering the limitations of the fieldwork we completed, it is important to recognize a bias in the demographic of people in our sample. Much of our observation was done in the immediate area of the stadium in the hours leading up to kickoff, and thus, a large percentage of the fans we spoke with and observed were attending that day's match. Ticket prices for the 2026 World Cup were the highest in the tournament's history, with the cheapest seats for the least expensive game in Seattle going for a few hundred dollars, and the two matches played by Team USA bottoming out above $3,000. This valuation is a significant barrier for many people, and undoubtedly skews the sample towards a wealthier tax bracket. It is important to note, however, that observation was also completed at multiple watch parties across the city, which were free to enter and thus possessed increased accessibility to a wider population.

Additional Bias

An additional sampling bias that should be considered lies within the population of Seattle- while many folks we spoke to attending the matches had travelled from out of town, it is certainly true that a larger number [cite AMP?] were locals, and this is even more true of the watch parties. Given this demographic information, replications of our study should take into account how behavior and expression may be altered by demographic patterns in the Seattle area- such as its overwhelmingly white population and generally left-wing political climate.

Sample Size and Generalizability

Another limitation on the comprehensiveness of our study is in sample size. There were only six total World Cup matches played in Seattle, and organized fieldwork was completed for just four of them. Some observations were completed at watch parties for matches taking place in other cities, including the World Cup Final on July 19th, but the vast majority were completed across the aforementioned four days, potentially impacting the generalizability of our findings.
