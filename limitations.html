Limitations

Project Limitations and Areas for Future Research

Like any short field study, this project's scope, sample, and point of view shaped what could and couldn't be observed.

Demographic Bias

When considering the limitations of the fieldwork we completed, it is important to recognize a bias in the demographics of our sample. Much of our observation was done in the immediate area of the stadium in the hours leading up to kickoff, and thus, a large percentage of the fans we spoke with and observed were attending that day’s match. Ticket prices for the 2026 World Cup were the highest in the tournament’s history (Lewis, 2026), with the cheapest seats for any game in Seattle going for a few hundred dollars, and the two matches played by Team USA bottoming out in the thousands (Al Jazeera). This valuation is a significant barrier for many people, and undoubtedly skews the sample towards a wealthier tax bracket. It is important to note, however, that observation was also completed at multiple watch parties across the city, which were free to enter and thus possessed increased accessibility to a wider population.

Additional Bias

An additional sampling bias that should be considered lies within the population of Seattle- while many folks we spoke to attending the matches had travelled from out of town, it is certainly the case that a larger number were locals, and this is even more true of the watch parties. Given this demographic information, replications of our observations should take into account how behavior and expression may be altered by demographic patterns in the Seattle area- such as its overwhelmingly white population and generally left-wing political climate.

Researcher positionality

It is finally relevant to acknowledge that the background and identity of our research team has the potential to create a bias in our fieldwork, photography, and observations, as well as the narrative derived from them. All four members of our research team are American; three have long backgrounds in soccer fandom, and two are consistent supporters of the USMNT. We are all University of Washington students and live in Seattle; both of these have an inherent impact on our perception of identity.

Sample Size and Generalizability

Another limitation on the comprehensiveness of our study is in sample size. There were only six total World Cup matches played in Seattle, and organized fieldwork was completed for just four of them. Some observations were completed at watch parties for matches taking place in other cities, including the World Cup Final on July 19th, but the vast majority were completed across the aforementioned four days, potentially impacting the generalizability of our findings.
