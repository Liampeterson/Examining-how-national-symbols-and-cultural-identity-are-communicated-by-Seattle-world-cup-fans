Introduction

This is a digital archive presenting four photo essays documenting how Seattle-based World Cup fans use flags, jerseys, chants, and costumes to express identity and belonging. Field notes, interviews, and photographs were collected during the 2026 Summer Institute in the Arts & Humanities (SIAH) research program based in Seattle, Washington, with fieldwork conducted at watch parties, fan marches, and match-day gatherings around the city.

F-01

Flags

Flags convert private loyalty into a public, legible claim — visible across a stadium concourse or a living room window long before anyone speaks. They mark territory and allegiance at a glance, making them one of the most immediate symbols fans use to announce who they support and where they belong.

J-01

Jerseys

Jerseys function as a wearable, everyday extension of identity — a second skin layered with names, numbers, and club or national crests. Because they're worn on ordinary days as much as match days, jerseys blur the line between spectator and belonging, turning identity into something lived rather than performed only in the moment.

R-01

Rituals

Chants, songs, and repeated fan traditions synchronize individual bodies into a single collective voice. These rituals matter because they turn isolated spectators into a coordinated group, reinforcing shared memory and belonging each time the same words and gestures are repeated together.

C-01

Costumes

Face paint, wigs, and elaborate dress push everyday fandom into deliberate performance and spectacle. Costumes matter here because they show identity being consciously staged, a fan choosing, often with real time and effort, exactly how their allegiance should be seen.
