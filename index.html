Introduction

Before the first whistle blows in Seattle Stadium, the energy on the street is already electric. Flags fly proudly along the waterfront, jerseys that have waited patiently in closets finally see daylight, and wild costumes spark conversations, turning strangers into a sudden community.

It’s been nearly thirty years since the men’s World Cup last touched American soil, and with every match comes another chance for the global soccer community to bring diverse life experiences and perspectives to our amazing city.

What we found is that fandom isn’t one fixed concept, but rather shifts with context, with audience, with who’s standing next to you. By looking closely at these elements and reading them against the frameworks scholars have built to understand fan identity, we hope to understand not just what fans do, but why fandom looks different depending on where you are.

Our result is a series of photo essays telling the story of Seattle’s fan experience during the 2026 FIFA World Cup, with analysis grounded equally in lived observation and in the ideas that help make sense of it. Every photo really does speak a thousand words, and behind each sits a question we were trying to answer. We focused our lens on four symbolic attributes that offer a window into a broader aspect of the fan experience: flags, jerseys, costumery, and rituals.

However, observation can only take a story so far. Alongside our field notes and photographs, we turned to existing scholarship on fandom and identity. The frames help explain why a flag draped over someone’s shoulder means something different at a bar than it does on a sidewalk, or why a chant is passed from parent to child, carrying the weight of ritual and belonging, rather than just pure noise. These theories don’t sit over our photographs as a detached academic layer; rather, we tried to let them move through the images themselves, giving language to what we were seeing on the ground.
