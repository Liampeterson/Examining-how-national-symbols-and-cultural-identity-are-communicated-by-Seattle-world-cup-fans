About

Project Overview

This digital archive presents four photo essays documenting how Seattle-based World Cup fans use flags, jerseys, ritual, and costumery to express identity and belonging. Field notes, interviews, and photographs were collected during the 2026 Summer Institute in the Arts & Humanities (SIAH) research program based in Seattle, Washington, with fieldwork conducted at watch parties, fan marches, and match-day gatherings around the city across June and July.

Since 2002, The University of Washington's Summer Institute in the Arts & Humanities has provided undergraduate students from all disciplines a fully-funded, faculty-guided research summer program promoting scholarship in the arts and humanities. Each year, the University selects around 18-21 students and a central theme to guide the research, and with the 23rd FIFA World Cup coming to Seattle, the 2026 theme was Seattle's World Cup: Storytelling through Community Mapping. The summer 2026 teaching team consisted of Caley Cook (Communications, Teaching Professor), Ron Krabill (Interdisciplinary Arts & Sciences, Professor), and Yasir Zaidan (International Studies, PhD Candidate).
